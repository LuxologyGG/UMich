Chair to Newman Lab

One chair, one bond. Slide from the chair view to looking straight down C1 to C2 and watch where every bond ends up on the paper Newman. Two example molecules, neither one a checkpoint item. Example 1 looks into the page, Example 2 looks out of it.

Turn your head with the slider. Dragging the model gives a free view; press a view button to snap back.

What you would draw on paper

    Four rules

    1. Axial is straight up or straight down in the chair, so it lands at 12 or 6 o'clock.
    2. Find the ring side next. If your eye looks into the page (toward the back edge), the paper's left is the Newman's left. If your eye looks out of the page (toward the front edge), left and right swap.
    3. The ring bond goes to the slanted spot on the ring side.
    4. The equatorial group takes the slanted spot on the other side, away from the ring.

    Guided run, about 5 minutes

    1. Example 1, Chair view. C1 (orange) is the right tip and C2 (blue) is the next carbon along the back edge. Your eye sits behind C1 and looks toward C2, which is deeper into the page.
    2. Drag the slider slowly to the end while you watch the axial Cl. It points straight up the whole way, so it lands at 12. C2's axial H points down the whole way, so it lands at 6.
    3. Look where the ring went. On paper the ring is to the left of C1, and since you looked into the page it stays on the left of the Newman. CH3, the equatorial group, goes to the slanted spot on the right.
    4. Switch to Example 2 and do the same slide. Now C1 is the left tip and C2 is on the front edge, so your eye looks out of the page, toward you. On paper the ring is to the right of C1, but on the Newman it lands on the left. Left and right swapped because you turned around.
    5. Press Ring flip in either example and slide again. Axial and equatorial swap, up and down stay the same.
